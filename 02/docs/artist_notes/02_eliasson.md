# Olafur Eliasson — Real / Virtual / Perceptual Environments

## Olafur Eliasson (b. 1967, Copenhagen/Reykjavik)

Danish-Icelandic artist and architect. His installations transform physical phenomena — light, water, temperature, optics — into immersive environments that foreground the viewer's perceptual experience.

**Key work referenced in Chapter 2:**
- *The Weather Project* (2003) — Tate Modern Turbine Hall. Constructed sun (monofrequency lights + mirrored ceiling) that filled the 5,000m² hall with amber light. Visitors lay on the floor watching their reflections, creating a collective, meditative experience. The simulated sun became more "real" than the real thing for its viewers.

**Relevance to Chapter 2:**
The chapter uses *The Weather Project* to illustrate the blurring of real and virtual in contemporary art. Eliasson's "sun" is neither natural nor digital — it is a carefully engineered simulation that produces authentic perceptual and emotional experience. This mirrors the *fluid matrix* concept: the virtual environment can achieve full presence without being physically real.

Eliasson also runs **Studio Olafur Eliasson** in Berlin — a large collaborative practice integrating architects, engineers, scientists, and artists, which anticipates the *Expanded Studio* model described in the chapter.

**Studio practice:**
Works such as *Waterfall* (2016, Versailles) and *In Real Life* (2019) continue the investigation into how constructed environments shape perception, increasingly integrating digital projection, sensors, and AI-driven responses.
