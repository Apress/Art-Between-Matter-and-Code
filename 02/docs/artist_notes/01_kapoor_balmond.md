# Anish Kapoor & Cecil Balmond — Digital Form-Finding

## Anish Kapoor (b. 1954, Mumbai)

British-Indian sculptor known for large-scale works exploring materiality, void, and reflective surfaces. His practice bridges physical fabrication and computational form-finding.

**Key works referenced in Chapter 2:**
- *Marsyas* (2002–03) — Tate Modern Turbine Hall. PVC membrane spanning 150m, engineered with Cecil Balmond using parametric structural modeling. The form was computationally derived before physical realization.
- *Cloud Gate* (2004) — Millennium Park, Chicago. Polished stainless steel, 10 × 20 × 13 m. The seamless reflective surface required advanced CNC machining and digital surface optimization.
- *Tall Tree and The Eye* (2009) — Guggenheim Bilbao. 73 reflective stainless-steel spheres. Repetition and variation within a generative system.

**Relevance to Chapter 2:**
Kapoor's mirrored sculptures embody generative variation — each sphere an instance of a repeating parametric rule. *Marsyas* demonstrates how computational modeling (structural simulation, parametric surface generation) is inseparable from artistic intent. This connects to the chapter's discussion of algorithmic form-finding and hybrid digital–physical workflows.

## Cecil Balmond (b. 1943, Sri Lanka)

Structural engineer and artist, former deputy chairman of Arup. Pioneer of "informal" structural design, using mathematical patterns and computational methods to generate non-linear architectural forms.

Collaboration with Kapoor on *Marsyas* as an example of the expanded studio: engineering computation, artistic vision, and digital fabrication integrated in a single workflow.
