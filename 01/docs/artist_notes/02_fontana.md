# Lucio Fontana (1899–1968)

**Movement:** Spatialism *(Spazialismo)*  
**Based in:** Milan, Buenos Aires  
**Relevant to:** Chapter 1 — §1.1.3

---

## Biography

Born in Rosario (Argentina) to Italian parents, Fontana studied at the Brera Academy under Adolfo Wildt. He divided his career between Milan and Buenos Aires, founding Spatialism in the mid-1940s. His radical gesture of cutting the canvas (*taglio*) made him one of the most influential artists of the 20th century.

## Key Works Referenced in Chapter 1

- *Concetti Spaziali, Attese* (1957–1968) — slashed monochromatic canvases
- *Ambienti Spaziali* — immersive spatial environments
- *Buchi* (holes series, from 1949)

## The Cut as Concept

Fontana's *taglio* (cut) is not destructive but generative:
- Opens the surface toward the void beyond
- Transforms the plane into a *threshold* between presence and absence
- Shifts sculpture from mass and permanence to *event and inscription*

**Computational reinterpretation:** The cut becomes a *boolean difference* — the void is not carved but calculated. The incision becomes a *rule*, a modular operation that organizes space in procedural terms.

## Conceptual Relevance

Fontana's trajectory — from canvas to spatial environments — mirrors the expansion from static 3D model to immersive metaverse space. His work laid the conceptual foundation for:
- Digital space as a *generative field*
- The void as a *parametric aperture*
- The artwork as *event* rather than object
