# Giancarlo Marchese & Alik Cavaliere

**Context:** Brera Academy, postwar decades (1950s–2000s)  
**Relevant to:** Chapter 1 — §1.1.4

---

## Giancarlo Marchese

**Born:** Milan, 1940s  
**Materials:** Glass, cast iron, raw industrial materials

### Practice

Marchese's sculpture does not aim to occupy space but to *question* it. Using essential materials and measured signs, he creates places of waiting and suspension — forms that invite an inward gaze rather than impose meaning.

His use of glass introduces transparency and reflective qualities that destabilize the viewer's sense of space. Structures are often open and fragmentary — thresholds between the visible and the invisible.

### Key Work

**S.N. 274, *Dialettica Formale*** (1993)  
Glass and cast iron, 230 × 160 × 40 cm  
Private collection, Milan  
*Photograph: Bart Herreman*

The frame — typically a decorative element — becomes protagonist. Instead of enclosing an image, it opens onto transparent, undulating glass traversed by reflections. An invitation to look *beyond*, to experience space as a mental field: unstable, never fully defined.

### Conceptual Relevance

Connects to Fontana's spatial explorations but offers an intimate, meditative interpretation. Anticipates contemporary immersive installation where the viewer inhabits, traverses, and reflects within the work.

---

## Alik Cavaliere (1926–1998)

**Materials:** Bronze, steel, plastic, natural forms (roots, branches, leaves)

### Practice

Cavaliere's work constructs *poetic scenographies* — ecological narratives populated by vegetal, surreal, and metaphysical structures. His installations transform space into a journey, a philosophical landscape where the viewer becomes an active participant.

The void between elements is integral to the work: a space to traverse, to inhabit.

### Key Work

***…E ne ha così assoluta certezza, quanto se n'abbia l'istessa natura*** (1966–67)  
Bronze, steel, plastic, 261 × 137 × 95 cm  
Private collection, Milan

### Conceptual Relevance

Cavaliere's deeply ecological and humanistic poetics forms an ideal bridge between late 20th-century sculpture and:
- Contemporary environmental storytelling
- Augmented reality
- Relational art

His installations anticipate the immersive, navigable spaces of the metaverse.

---

## Shared Legacy

Both artists studied under **Marino Marini** at the Brera Academy. Both expand sculpture toward narrative and environmental dimensions, anticipating:
- Immersive digital environments (Chapter 9–10)
- The concept of the artwork as *space to inhabit*, not merely *object to observe*
