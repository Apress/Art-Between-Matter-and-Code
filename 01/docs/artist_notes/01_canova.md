# Antonio Canova (1757–1822)

**Movement:** Neoclassicism  
**Based in:** Rome, Venice, Possagno  
**Relevant to:** Chapter 1 — §1.1.1

---

## Biography

Born in Possagno (Treviso), Canova became the leading sculptor of the Neoclassical movement. Trained in Venice, he settled in Rome in 1781, where he achieved international fame. He was directly linked to the Brera Academy through his influence on its reforms during the Napoleonic period.

## Key Works Referenced in Chapter 1

- *Psyche Revived by Cupid's Kiss* (1787–1793) — marble, 155 × 168 cm, Louvre, Paris
- *Napoleon as Mars the Peacemaker* (plaster 1802–06; bronze cast 1811) — Brera Academy courtyard, Milan
- *Terracotta sketch for Cupid and Psyche* — Museo Correr, Venice

## Working Method

Canova's process moved through three distinct phases:
1. **Clay model** — tactile sketch, immediate capture of idea and gesture
2. **Plaster model** — prototype, archive, planning space; preserved gesture for translation into final material
3. **Marble or bronze** — final execution with precision pointing machines

This workflow is structurally analogous to modern digital pipelines: sketch → 3D model → fabrication.

## Conceptual Relevance

Canova's Neoclassicism elevated the *mental project* (the idea, the design) as autonomous from physical execution — anticipating the logic of digital creation, where an artwork is conceived as code or virtual model before becoming material. His pursuit of mathematical proportion and ideal beauty anticipates algorithmic and generative logics.

## Key Institutions

- **Gipsoteca Canoviana, Possagno** — the most complete collection of Canova's plaster models; expanded by Carlo Scarpa in 1957
- **Brera Academy, Milan** — his bronze Napoleon marks the entrance to the Cortile Napoleonico
- **Museo Correr, Venice**
- **Louvre, Paris**
