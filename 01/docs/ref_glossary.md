# Glossary of Key Terms
### Supplementary material for Chapter 1 — *Art Between Matter and Code*

---

## Art Movements & Concepts

**Neoclassicism**
Artistic movement (c. 1760–1850) inspired by the art and culture of ancient Greece and Rome. In sculpture, it pursued ideals of proportion, harmony, and timeless beauty. Key figure: Antonio Canova. Conceptually relevant because it elevated the *mental project* as autonomous from physical execution — a precursor to digital design logic.

**Spatialism** *(Spazialismo)*
Movement founded by Lucio Fontana (1946–). Prioritizes space, light, and gesture over traditional material concerns. The iconic slashed canvases (*Concetti Spaziali*) open the surface toward the void, redefining sculpture as event rather than mass. Foundational concept for understanding digital space as a *generative field*.

**Arte Povera**
Italian movement (1960s–70s) using humble, everyday materials to challenge the commodification of art. Relevant as a counterpoint to digital dematerialization — it reasserted materiality at the moment when immateriality became thinkable.

**Apollonian / Dionysian**
Nietzsche's opposition between *Apollonian* art (sculpture: order, clarity, form, control) and *Dionysian* art (music: chaos, flux, emotion, emergence). In the digital context: algorithmic control (Apollonian) versus generative unpredictability (Dionysian). Chapter 1 frames contemporary digital practice as a tension between the two.

**Aura** *(Walter Benjamin)*
The unique, unrepeatable presence of an original artwork in a specific place and time. Benjamin argued that mechanical reproduction destroys aura. In the digital context: NFTs and blockchain attempt to re-establish aura for digital works by certifying uniqueness. See also: Chapter 4.

## Sculptural Techniques

**Clay modeling**
The primary exploratory phase in traditional sculpture. Allows rapid, tactile experimentation with form. Analogous to *sketching* in digital workflows — the direct capture of the sculptor's initial idea.

**Plaster cast** *(calco in gesso)*
Both a preservation technique and an autonomous artistic moment. Captures the immediacy of gesture. Functioned as prototype and archive. The Gipsoteca (plaster cast gallery) is the physical equivalent of a version-controlled digital repository.

**Gipsoteca**
A collection of plaster casts, typically from a sculptor's studio. Notable examples: Gipsoteca Canoviana (Possagno, expanded by Carlo Scarpa in 1957), Gipsoteca Bistolfi (Casale Monferrato), Gipsoteca della Brera (Milan).

**Bronze casting**
Lost-wax or sand casting of metal. Traditionally the final, "official" form of a sculpture. Equivalent in durability and authority to a signed, limited-edition digital print.

## Digital & Computational Terms

**Mesh**
A 3D digital object made of vertices, edges, and faces (polygons). The digital equivalent of a clay model — the fundamental building block of 3D sculpture in software like Blender.

**Polygon** *(poly)*
The basic face unit of a 3D mesh. A lower poly count = simpler form; higher poly count = more detail. Equivalent to the level of refinement in a clay or plaster model.

**Boolean operation**
A 3D modeling technique that combines or subtracts volumes mathematically. *Union*, *intersection*, *difference*. The computational reinterpretation of Fontana's cut: the void is not carved but calculated.

**Parametric modeling**
Design approach in which forms are defined by adjustable parameters (values, rules, relationships) rather than fixed shapes. Changes to a parameter automatically update the entire form. Enables the "open possibilities" described in Chapter 1.

**Procedural generation**
Creating content (geometry, texture, animation) through algorithms and rules rather than manual modeling. Explored in depth in Chapter 3.

**Digital fabrication**
Manufacturing physical objects from digital models. Includes 3D printing (FDM, SLA, SLS), CNC milling, laser cutting. Closes the loop between virtual and material — see Chapter 6.

**Photogrammetry**
Technique for creating 3D digital models from multiple photographs. Used to digitize sculptures, archaeological objects, and spaces. Bridges physical and virtual.

**Generative AI** *(in art)*
AI systems (Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, DALL-E, ComfyUI) that produce images, video, or 3D content from text or image prompts. In Chapter 1, used to reinterpret Fontana's spatial cuts in a computational register.

**Metaverse**
A networked virtual environment where digital objects and spaces exist persistently. Explored as an exhibition space in Chapter 9.

## Key Institutions

**Accademia di Belle Arti di Brera** (founded 1776, Milan)
One of Italy's oldest and most prestigious art academies. The institutional context of the book. Its sculptural heritage runs from Canova's influence through Fontana, Marchese, Cavaliere, Marini, to contemporary digital practice.

**Brera Academy Virtual Lab** (founded 2008)
Digital sculpture and architecture laboratory within Brera, co-founded by Stefania Albertini and Gianpiero Moioli. Pioneering context for hybrid physical-digital artistic practice.
