## David Hockney (b. 1937, Bradford, UK)

David Hockney is one of the most significant British artists of the twentieth and twenty-first centuries, known for his restless investigation of perception, colour, and pictorial space. His career spans traditional oil painting, Cubist-influenced photomontage, theatrical design, and digital media — each phase driven by the question of how the eye moves through an image and how time can be inscribed in a single picture.

### iPad Drawings and *The Yosemite Suite*

Beginning around 2009, Hockney adopted the iPhone and then the iPad as drawing tools, producing thousands of works in the Brushes application. The iPad drawings — including *The Yosemite Suite* (2010) and extensive floral studies — preserve the immediacy of the hand and the luminosity of light-emitting screens. Hockney described the device as "the most intimate sketchbook I've ever had": a surface on which pigment behaves like light rather than matter.

Several exhibitions — most notably the large-scale retrospective at the Royal Academy of Arts (2012) — presented iPad drawings projected at monumental scale or printed on paper. This translation from screen to print directly parallels the Chapter 5 discussion of scale independence in digital painting: the work is created at one resolution and realized at another, with no loss of gestural quality.

### Why Hockney in Chapter 5

Chapter 5 (§5.2.2) invokes Hockney's iPad drawings as a modern precedent that demonstrates how manual expression — once bound to paper or canvas — can find continuity in a digital interface. Key parallels with the hybrid practices described in the chapter:

| Hockney's iPad practice | Chapter 5 concept |
|------------------------|-------------------|
| Hand gesture preserved in pixels | Continuity of manual gesture in digital environments |
| Screen as luminous, immaterial surface | Digital painting as "immaterial, but present" |
| Monumental prints from small-screen originals | Scale independence via AI upscaling |
| Rapid iteration across thousands of studies | Multiplicity of outcomes from a single digital matrix |

### Selected Works Referenced

- *The Yosemite Suite* (2010) — iPad drawings on paper, Royal Academy of Arts
- Floral iPad studies (2009–ongoing) — exhibited internationally
- *A Bigger Picture* (2011) — large-scale landscape paintings combining observation and digital studies; Royal Academy retrospective

### Hockney and Tradition

Hockney is an acute observer of art history. His book *Secret Knowledge* (2001) argued that Renaissance masters used optical devices — camera obscura, camera lucida — as drawing aids, making him one of the few artists to actively research the history of technological assistance in art. This historical sensitivity makes him a fitting reference for Chapter 5's argument: that digital tools are the latest instance of technology expanding the painter's capabilities rather than replacing them.

> *"All painting is digital in the sense that it's made with fingers — digitus."*  
> — David Hockney, as paraphrased in multiple interviews
