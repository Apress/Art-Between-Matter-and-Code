## Hans Op de Beeck (b. 1969, Turnhout, Belgium)

Hans Op de Beeck is a Belgian multidisciplinary artist working across sculpture, installation, film, drawing, and writing. His practice is unified by a distinctive visual language: monochromatic grey environments — figures, interiors, landscapes — rendered in materials that hover between presence and absence, reality and dream. His work is held in major international collections and has been exhibited at the Venice Biennale, Documenta, and leading museums worldwide.

### The Monochrome Method

Op de Beeck's sculptural and installation works are characteristically coated in a uniform, matte grey or dusty white. This flattening of colour eliminates the visual hierarchy that colour normally establishes — weight, warmth, familiarity — leaving only form, light, and shadow. The effect is simultaneously intimate and estranging: objects and figures are immediately recognizable yet somehow suspended outside time.

His materials are deliberately diverse (resin, polyester, bronze, wood, fabric) but made visually equivalent by the monochromatic finish. The grey is not a neutral choice; it evokes:

- the tone of memory, dream, and nostalgia
- the surface of plaster casts and archaeological casts
- the visual grammar of architectural models

### *Nocturnal Journey* (2025)

The exhibition referenced directly in Chapter 5 (§5.4.4). It presented **39 life-size sculptures** derived from:

- **3D digital scanning** of everyday objects, furniture, and human figures
- **Manual refinement** of the resulting grey resin sculptures by hand after printing/casting

Op de Beeck's method exemplifies what the chapter calls the "poetry of the digital-to-physical chain": the scanner reproduces reality with objective precision, but the artist then reinterprets, organizes, and rewrites it sculpturally through the manual finishing of each resin piece. As critic Guendalina Perelli observed:

> *"The scanner reproduces reality, but the artist reinterprets, organizes, and rewrites it sculpturally; the result is not a cold copy, but a poetic echo, filled with temporality and emotion."*

### Why Op de Beeck in Chapter 5

Chapter 5 uses Op de Beeck as evidence that digital scanning and 3D technology do not necessarily lead to "shiny, high-tech aesthetics" — that, on the contrary, they can renew timeless sculptural themes (vanitas, dream, memory) through contemporary means.

| Op de Beeck's practice | Chapter 5 concept |
|-----------------------|-------------------|
| 3D scanning as starting point | Digital capture preserves gestural traces of the real |
| Manual refinement of digital output | Human intervention restores poetry to algorithmic precision |
| Monochrome finish equalizes all objects | Poetic reinterpretation over literal reproduction |
| *Nocturnal Journey*: life-size scanned figures | Full-scale immersive sculptural environments from data |
| Grey resin as material choice | Matter as vehicle for temporality and emotional resonance |

The key argument: **authorship emerges in the overall composition and in the material choices**, not in the scanning itself. The scanner is a tool of acquisition; the artist remains the author of the final form.

### Contrast with Other Digital Sculpture Approaches

Chapter 5 implicitly positions Op de Beeck against artists like Julius Popp and Michael Hansmeyer, who push digital sculpture toward algorithmic baroque complexity — dense recursive geometries that challenge human intuition. Op de Beeck moves in the opposite direction: toward the essential, the rarefied, the meditative. Both positions are valid outcomes of the same digital toolkit; the artist's vision determines the aesthetic destination.

### Selected Works

- *The Staging* (2019) — monochrome installation of a banquet table with 28 life-size silicone figures; S.M.A.K., Ghent
- *Location (6)* (2012) — large-scale grey interior installation; Yorkshire Sculpture Park
- *Staging Silence* (2009, 2013, 2022) — multi-episode film of model-scale theatrical sets constructed and disassembled; widely exhibited
- *Nocturnal Journey* (2025) — 39 life-size scanned and resin-finished sculptures
