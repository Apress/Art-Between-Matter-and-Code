## Anish Kapoor (b. 1954, Mumbai, India; based London, UK)

Anish Kapoor is one of the most internationally prominent sculptors working today, known for large-scale works that explore phenomenological themes of void, presence, concavity, and the behavior of light on highly finished surfaces. His practice spans site-specific public commissions, gallery installations, and philosophical investigations of material and form.

### Cloud Gate (2006, Chicago)

*Cloud Gate* — popularly known as "The Bean" — is Kapoor's most widely recognized work: a 110-tonne elliptical sculpture in polished stainless steel installed in Millennium Park, Chicago. The surface was assembled from 168 individual curved steel plates, welded and polished to seamless smoothness with a tolerance of less than 1 mm. The fabrication required advanced CNC milling and digital surface modeling to translate Kapoor's organic form into manufacturable sections.

The work exemplifies precision large-scale digital fabrication: the sculpture's form could not have been realized without computer-controlled cutting and forming of the steel plates, and the reflective surface (which distorts and multiplies the surrounding cityscape) depends on the exactness of that fabrication.

### Relevance to Chapter 6

Chapter 6 (§6.1) references *Cloud Gate* as an example of CNC milling and precision digital fabrication at monumental scale — a different end of the fabrication spectrum from desktop 3D printing, but governed by the same virtual-to-physical logic: digital model → precision toolpaths → physical realization. The work demonstrates that the fabrication chain described in Chapter 6 scales from a 15-cm resin print to a 110-tonne public sculpture.

## Factum Arte (founded 2001, Madrid, Spain)

Factum Arte is a Madrid-based digital preservation and fabrication studio founded by Adam Lowe. The studio specializes in ultra-high-resolution scanning, digital archiving, and CNC-fabricated facsimiles of art works and heritage sites. Their work occupies a philosophically rich zone: facsimiles are not forgeries (no claim of originality) but physical embodiments of digital data, produced at the same scale and with equivalent surface detail as the original.

### The Wedding at Cana (2007)

Factum Arte's most celebrated project is the facsimile of Paolo Veronese's *Wedding at Cana* (1563), originally removed from the refectory of San Giorgio Maggiore in Venice by Napoleon in 1797 and now in the Louvre, Paris. Factum Arte scanned the original at ultra-high resolution, fabricated a 1:1 facsimile using a combination of pigment printing and CNC-textured surface, and returned it to Venice in 2007 — restoring a 500-year-old spatial relationship between the painting and the room for which it was painted.

### Tutankhamun Burial Chamber Facsimile (2014)

For the Factum Foundation's "Theban Mapping Project," Factum Arte produced a complete facsimile of the burial chamber of Tutankhamun in the Valley of the Kings. The chamber was scanned using structured light and photogrammetry; the facsimile was CNC-milled in high-density foam, hand-painted, and installed near Luxor as a heritage tourism alternative — reducing visitor pressure on the original tomb.
