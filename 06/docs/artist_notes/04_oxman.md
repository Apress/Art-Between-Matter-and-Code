## Neri Oxman (b. 1976, Haifa, Israel; MIT Media Lab, Mediated Matter Group)

Neri Oxman is an architect, designer, and researcher whose work lies at the intersection of computational design, additive manufacturing, materials science, and biology. As founder and director of the MIT Media Lab's Mediated Matter group (2010–2019), she pioneered the field of "material ecology" — a design approach in which structures are conceived as organisms: multi-functional, gradient in composition, and grown rather than assembled. Her practice is one of the most rigorous explorations of what additive manufacturing makes possible when computation, biology, and fabrication are genuinely integrated.

### Silk Pavilion I (2013)

*Silk Pavilion I* is Oxman's most widely cited work and the primary reference in Chapter 6. The structure consists of a primary CNC-fabricated silk scaffold — a geodesic dome of continuous silk thread deposited by a six-axis robotic arm — onto which approximately 6,500 live silkworms were placed. The silkworms completed the surface by spinning their cocoons across the scaffold, depositing silk fiber in patterns determined by the combination of the digital scaffold geometry and the worms' biological response to light gradients and surface curvature.

The result is a hybrid structure in which digital geometry and biological behavior co-produce the final material form. The scaffold was designed computationally to guide the worms toward areas requiring denser silk coverage (structural loads) and lighter coverage (light transmission), mimicking the functional gradients found in natural silk structures.

*Silk Pavilion II* (2020) extended the concept by replacing the silkworms' surface deposition with a high-resolution multi-material 3D print that simulated the biological silk gradient — demonstrating that the formal logic discovered through biology could be computationally modeled and manufactured without live organisms.

### Mushtari (2014)

*Mushtari* is a wearable 3D-printed garment whose interior channels carry living organisms (photosynthetic microorganisms, bioluminescent bacteria) in a continuous fluid network. The form references a fantasy interpretation of the planet Jupiter's cloud systems. Fabricated using Stratasys PolyJet multi-material printing (Objet Connex), the translucent and flexible channels allow light to pass through the living contents. *Mushtari* demonstrated that 3D printing could produce structures that host biology — moving from inert object to living system.

### Aguahoja I (2018)

*Aguahoja I* is a 4.6-metre biopolymer pavilion fabricated by a custom robotic deposition system that prints with water-based composites of cellulose, chitosan, and pectin — materials found in plant cell walls and insect exoskeletons. The gradient composition of the structure responds to the pavilion's structural loads and desired optical properties. *Aguahoja I* establishes a direct precedent for WASP's ceramic printing philosophy: extrusion-based deposition of a natural material, with machine-controlled variation in material properties across the structure.

### Relevance to Chapter 6

Chapter 6 (§6.3) invokes Oxman in the context of WASP ceramic printing and robotic fabrication. The philosophical parallel is precise: both practices use additive deposition of natural materials (silk/clay/biopolymers) guided by digital toolpaths that encode functional gradients. WASP's CEREBRO slicer modulates clay extrusion speed and layer height in response to structural needs, echoing the variable-density approach pioneered in Oxman's work at Mediated Matter.
