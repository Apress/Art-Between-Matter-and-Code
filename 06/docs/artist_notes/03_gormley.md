## Antony Gormley (b. 1950, London, UK)

Antony Gormley is one of the most significant British sculptors of the post-war period, internationally recognized for his sustained investigation of the human body as a site of memory, consciousness, and collective experience. For over four decades he has used his own body as the primary matrix for sculpture — first through direct casting in lead, later through scanning and digital fabrication — producing works that range from intimate gallery objects to monumental public installations spanning entire cities.

### Body Casting as Core Practice

Gormley's practice originated in the early 1980s with a radical methodological decision: to use his own body, cast from life, as the origin of every sculpture. Early works encase full-body casts in lead — *Three Ways: Mould, Tube and Casement* (1981–82) — producing surfaces that preserve the indexical trace of the body while transforming it into a dense, impenetrable material. The cast is simultaneously intimate (it is his body) and alienated (it is sealed, opaque, inert).

This insistence on the body as both subject and material origin gives Gormley's practice its philosophical consistency across radical changes in technique and scale.

### Digital Scanning and Re-Materialization

From the 1990s onward, Gormley incorporated 3D scanning of his body into the fabrication chain. Laser scans of the standing, sitting, or crouching body generate point clouds that are processed into mesh models; these digital representations are then output as sculptures through casting (iron, steel, lead) or CNC-fabricated molds. The scan introduces a layer of translation between the body and the material — the sculpture is no longer an indexical touch of plaster on skin, but an information structure reconstituted in metal.

Works from the *Expansion Field* series and the *Model* series (2012) use scanned body data at varying scales and with differing degrees of resolution, producing body forms whose surfaces record the geometry of measurement rather than the texture of skin. Some works in the *Host* series use the scan to drive industrial casting processes, resulting in iron figures produced in multiple identical instances — questioning uniqueness and presence in ways that parallel debates around digital reproduction in photography and video.

### Relevance to Chapter 6

Chapter 6 (§6.1) references Gormley as a key figure in the scan → re-materialization chain: the body functions as a physical matrix that is digitized and then re-embodied in a different material, at a different scale, through a different fabrication process. This is one of the earliest and most sustained artistic uses of the virtual-to-physical workflow in fine art — and one of the most philosophically charged, since the "virtual" original is the artist's own living body.
