# Chapter 6 — Glossary of Key Terms

> **Art Between Matter and Code** · Gianpiero Moioli · Apress 2025  
> Reference companion to Chapter 6: *From Virtuality to Matter: 3D Printing and Material Translation*

---

## A

**Additive Manufacturing**  
Umbrella term for all fabrication processes that build objects layer by layer from digital data, in contrast to subtractive methods (milling, turning) that remove material from a block. Encompasses FDM, SLA, MSLA, SLS, PolyJet, binder jetting, and ceramic extrusion. Also known as 3D printing. International standard: ISO/ASTM 52900. The defining technology of Chapter 6's virtual-to-physical chain.

**Anycubic Slicer Next**  
Proprietary slicing software developed by Anycubic for their range of FDM and resin printers (including the Photon M5 MSLA). Converts 3D mesh files (STL, OBJ, 3MF) into layer-by-layer print instructions. Key features include anti-aliasing for resin prints, support auto-generation, and direct file transfer to the printer. Used in §6.5.2 for the *Little Metaphysical Piazza* resin workflow.

---

## B

**Bed Adhesion**  
The bonding between the first layer of a 3D print and the build platform. Poor adhesion causes warping or print failure, especially with ABS and large flat FDM objects. Solutions include heated beds, PEI sheets, brim/raft structures, and enclosures. Anycubic Kobra 2 Max uses automatic 25-point leveling for consistent first-layer adhesion.

**Binder Jetting**  
Additive manufacturing process in which a liquid binding agent is selectively deposited onto a powder bed (sand, metal, or ceramic). The bound layers are cured and sintered to produce the final part. Used for full-color sandstone prints (3D Systems ProJet CJP) and metal parts. Michael Hansmeyer's *Digital Grotesque* (2013) employed a related powder-based process.

---

## C

**CDM — Certified Digital Manifest**  
JSON record containing the SHA-256 fingerprint of a digital file, timestamp, author metadata, and optional chain link to previous versions. Defined in §4.3.4 (Chapter 4). In Chapter 6 it appears as the provenance anchor for fabricated artworks: the source STL/OBJ and slicing parameters are archived with a CDM prior to printing, providing a tamper-evident record of the digital original. See also `certify_asset.py` (Chapter 4 scripts).

**CEREBRO**  
WASP's proprietary slicer software for ceramic and large-format 3D printing. Designed specifically for the WASP Delta 40100 Clay and BigDelta series. CEREBRO generates toolpaths optimized for clay rheology: variable layer height, print speed modulation based on clay viscosity, and support strategies for open forms. Discussed in §6.3.2.

**Clay Printing**  
Extrusion-based 3D printing using ceramic materials — primarily stoneware clay, earthenware, or porcelain — delivered through a pneumatic or auger-driven extruder mounted on a delta-type robotic frame. The WASP Delta 40100 Clay can print objects up to 1 m diameter × 1 m height. Clay prints require controlled drying to prevent cracking, followed by bisque firing and optional glazing. Discussed in §6.3.1.

---

## D

**Digital Grotesque**  
Architectural installation by Michael Hansmeyer and Benjamin Dillenburger (2013): two 3.2-metre sandstone columns generated by a recursive subdivision algorithm that produces approximately 260 million individual facets at 16 µm resolution. Fabricated using binder jetting. *Digital Grotesque II* (2017) scaled the process to a full immersive grotto. Represents the extreme of geometric complexity enabled by additive manufacturing. Referenced in §6.2.

**DLP — Digital Light Processing**  
Resin 3D printing variant that uses a digital projector to cure an entire layer simultaneously, rather than a scanning laser (SLA) or masked LCD panel (MSLA). Faster than laser SLA for small-to-medium objects; resolution depends on projector pixel pitch. Often grouped with SLA and MSLA as photopolymer resin printing.

---

## F

**FDM / FFF — Fused Deposition Modeling / Fused Filament Fabrication**  
The most widely adopted consumer 3D printing process. A thermoplastic filament (PLA, PETG, ABS, TPU) is heated above its glass transition temperature and extruded through a nozzle, depositing material layer by layer on a heated bed. Dimensional accuracy: ±0.1–0.2 mm. Typical layer heights: 0.08–0.3 mm. Key parameters: nozzle temperature (215°C for PLA), bed temperature (60°C for PLA), print speed (40–150 mm/s), infill pattern and percentage. Author's *Dragon in the Forest* used FDM on an Anycubic Kobra 2 Max in three interlocking PLA segments. Discussed in §6.2.1.

---

## G

**G-code**  
Low-level machine instruction language used by most FDM and some resin printers. Generated automatically by slicers (Cura, PrusaSlicer, Anycubic Slicer Next). Contains coordinates (X, Y, Z), extruder movements (E), temperature commands (M104, M140), fan control, and retraction parameters. Understanding G-code allows manual fine-tuning of critical print sections.

**Grasshopper**  
Visual programming plugin for Rhinoceros 3D, used for parametric and algorithmic design. In Chapter 6, Grasshopper is paired with the KUKA|prc plugin to generate robotic toolpaths for large-format 3D printing and CNC fabrication. Referenced in §6.3.3.

---

## I

**Infill**  
The internal structure of a 3D-printed object, generated automatically by the slicer between the outer walls and top/bottom surfaces. Expressed as a percentage (0% = hollow, 100% = solid). Common patterns: grid, gyroid, honeycomb, lightning, cubic. Gyroid infill provides optimal strength-to-weight ratio for organic sculptural forms. A 15% gyroid infill is used for the *Dragon in the Forest* segments.

**Iterative Prototyping**  
Design methodology in which a rapid cycle of model → print → physical test → digital refinement is repeated until the work meets aesthetic and structural criteria. The short print time of FDM desktop printers (hours rather than days) makes iterative prototyping accessible to individual artists. Discussed in §6.4.

---

## K

**KUKA|prc**  
Grasshopper plugin developed by the Association for Robots in Architecture that translates parametric geometry into KUKA robot arm motion programs (KRL code). Enables precise control of end-effector position and orientation for large-format 3D printing, CNC milling, and multi-axis fabrication. Used in §6.3.3 for robotic ceramic printing workflows.

---

## L

**Lost-Wax Casting**  
Traditional bronze casting technique adapted for digitally originated sculptures. Workflow: digital model → FDM PLA master → silicone mold → wax copy → ceramic shell (investment) → bronze pour → chasing. Digital shrinkage compensation: +2.0% applied to the STL file before printing to account for bronze contraction during cooling. Observed failure modes: surface porosity, extreme warping. Cross-reference: §5.5.1 and App. F (Chapter 5). In Chapter 6, lost-wax casting appears as an output path from the fabrication workflow.

---

## M

**Material Translation**  
The conceptual shift from digital information — a mesh, a set of slicing parameters, a G-code file — to physical matter with weight, texture, thermal properties, and surface imperfection. Material translation is never neutral: each printing technology introduces its own aesthetic character (FDM layer lines, resin surface smoothness, clay cracking). Chapter 6 frames this shift as a creative decision rather than a mere technical step. Central theme of §6.1.

**Modular Assembly**  
Design strategy for large-format 3D printing in which the total form is divided into interlocking segments, each within the build volume of the printer, reassembled after printing. Requires precise tolerance management (0.2–0.4 mm clearance for PLA-to-PLA snap fits) and alignment features (pins, dovetails, or threaded inserts). Author's *Dragon in the Forest* uses three interlocking PLA segments printed on the Kobra 2 Max. Discussed in §6.5.1.

**MSLA — Masked Stereolithography**  
Resin 3D printing process that uses an LCD panel as a dynamic mask to cure photopolymer resin layer by layer with UV light. The mask switches between opaque and transparent pixels to define each layer's geometry. XY resolution determined by LCD pixel pitch: the Anycubic Photon M5 achieves 16–50 µm XY resolution. MSLA is faster than laser SLA for production volumes because the entire layer is exposed simultaneously. Used for the author's *Little Metaphysical Piazza* (§6.5.2).

---

## N

**Non-manifold Geometry**  
Mesh condition in which one or more edges are shared by more than two faces, or where edges have no adjacent faces (boundary edges in a supposedly closed solid). Non-manifold geometry makes a mesh "non-watertight" and prevents slicers from generating valid toolpaths. Detection: 3D Print Toolbox → Check All; or Edit Mode → Select → Select All by Trait → Non-Manifold. Repair tools: Fill Holes, Delete Loose, Merge by Distance. See also `print_ready_checker.py` (§6.2.5).

---

## O

**Overhang**  
Geometry that extends beyond a critical angle from the vertical build axis — typically 45° — and cannot be self-supporting during layer deposition. FDM overhangs beyond 45° require support structures or orientation optimization. SLA/MSLA builds inverted (bottom-up), so overhang management differs. SLS requires no support structures due to the surrounding unsintered powder bed. Overhang analysis is available in all major slicers and in Blender's 3D Print Toolbox.

---

## P

**PETG — Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol**  
FDM filament combining the ease of printing of PLA with improved toughness, chemical resistance, and heat resistance compared to standard PLA. Food-safe grades available. Print temperature: 230–250°C nozzle, 70–85°C bed. Less prone to warping than ABS. Preferred for functional parts and outdoor or food-contact applications.

**PLA — Polylactic Acid**  
The most common FDM filament, derived from renewable starch (corn, sugarcane). Biodegradable under industrial composting conditions. Print temperature: 190–220°C nozzle, 50–60°C bed (or cold bed with adhesion aid). Dimensional accuracy: ±0.1–0.2 mm. Excellent detail reproduction; lower impact strength than PETG or ABS. Used in the author's *Dragon in the Forest* (Kobra 2 Max, three modular PLA segments). Discussed in §6.2.1.

**PolyJet**  
Multi-material inkjet 3D printing process developed by Stratasys (J750, J55 printers). Photopolymer droplets are jetted from multiple heads simultaneously and cured by UV light in each pass, enabling gradients between different materials (rigid/flexible, opaque/transparent) within a single print. XY resolution: 600 DPI (42 µm). Supports up to seven simultaneous materials. Used for complex bio-inspired structures and product prototypes. Discussed in §6.2.4.

---

## R

**Resin (Photopolymer)**  
UV-curable liquid polymer used in SLA, MSLA (LCD), and DLP 3D printing. Formulations range from standard resins (brittle, high detail) to ABS-like, flexible, castable (for lost-wax), and biocompatible (dental, medical) variants. Post-processing requires UV washing (IPA or Anycubic Wash & Cure) and secondary UV curing to complete polymerization. Anycubic Photon M5 resin achieves 16–50 µm XY resolution. Discussed in §6.2.2.

**Robotic Fabrication**  
CNC-controlled multi-axis robotic arm (KUKA, ABB, Fanuc) used as a motion platform for 3D printing nozzles, milling spindles, or welding torches. Unlike gantry-type 3D printers, robots offer six degrees of freedom, enabling non-planar printing, curved layer deposition, and very large build volumes (limited only by the robot's reach envelope). WASP's CRANE system and the KUKA|prc workflow are the Chapter 6 examples. Discussed in §6.3.3.

---

## S

**SLA — Stereolithography**  
The original resin 3D printing process, invented by Chuck Hull in 1984. A UV laser traces each layer on the surface of a photopolymer resin vat, curing it selectively. Top-down SLA (Form 3, Formlabs): laser cures from above, part builds downward. Bottom-up SLA: laser or light source below the vat, part pulled upward. High accuracy (XY: ~25–140 µm depending on laser spot size) and smooth surface quality. Preferred for jewelry, dental, and fine art applications. Discussed in §6.2.2.

**SLS — Selective Laser Sintering**  
Powder-bed additive manufacturing process in which a CO₂ laser selectively sinters (fuses) nylon (PA12, PA11) or other powder material layer by layer. No support structures are required — the surrounding powder acts as a support medium. Dimensional accuracy: ±0.3 mm; wall thickness minimum: ~0.8 mm. Parts emerge gray and porous; post-processing options include dyeing, polishing, or painting. Used for complex interlocking structures and functional prototypes. Discussed in §6.2.3.

**Slicing**  
The computational process of converting a 3D mesh (STL, OBJ, 3MF) into a layer-by-layer sequence of machine instructions (G-code for FDM; proprietary formats for resin and SLS). Slicers calculate wall paths, infill patterns, support structures, layer height, print speed, and temperature profiles. Key slicers in Chapter 6: Anycubic Slicer Next (for Photon M5 and Kobra 2 Max), WASP CEREBRO (for ceramic printing), Cura (open source, broad printer support). Discussed in §6.1.1.

**Support Structures**  
Temporary geometry automatically generated by slicers to support overhanging regions during printing. FDM supports: tree-type or linear, printed in the same or a soluble material (PVA, HIPS). Resin supports: thin pillars with conical tips. SLS: no supports needed. Post-processing involves mechanical removal, sanding, and surface finishing of support contact points. Discussed in §6.4.

---

## T

**Tolerance**  
Dimensional accuracy of a fabrication process, expressed as the maximum expected deviation from the nominal dimension. FDM: ±0.1–0.2 mm (well-calibrated desktop printer). Resin SLA/MSLA: XY 16–50 µm (pixel pitch), Z ~25–50 µm (layer height). SLS: ±0.3 mm. PolyJet: ±0.1 mm. Tolerance determines fit between interlocking parts: PLA-to-PLA snap fits require 0.2–0.4 mm clearance per side.

---

## V

**Virtual-to-Physical Workflow**  
The complete chain of processes transforming a digital 3D model into a physical object: modeling or scanning → mesh repair and validation → slicing → printing → post-processing → finishing and certification. Each step introduces material and aesthetic decisions. Chapter 6 frames this chain as a form of translation — not from language to language, but from information to matter. Discussed throughout Chapter 6, especially §6.1 and §6.1.1.

---

## W

**Wall Thickness**  
The minimum thickness of a printed shell or wall, determined by the printing process and nozzle/laser diameter. FDM minimum: 2 mm (2× nozzle diameter for a 0.4 mm nozzle, ×2.5 recommended for strength). Resin minimum: 1 mm. SLS minimum: 0.8 mm. Below these thresholds, walls may fail to print or be structurally fragile. Checked via 3D Print Toolbox → Thickness or `print_ready_checker.py`.

**WASP**  
Italian manufacturer (World's Advanced Saving Project, based in Massa Lombarda, Emilia-Romagna) specializing in large-format clay, cement, and earth 3D printers. Key products: Delta 40100 Clay (400 mm diameter × 1000 mm height, clay/porcelain extrusion), BigDelta 12M (12-metre tall construction-scale earth printer). WASP developed the CEREBRO slicer specifically for ceramic toolpath optimization. Their philosophy aligns digital fabrication with sustainable and traditional ceramic craft. Discussed in §6.3.
