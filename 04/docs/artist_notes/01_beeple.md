# Beeple (Mike Winkelmann)
## §4.2.3 — Licensing, Authorship, and the NFT Market

> Artist note for *Art Between Matter and Code* · Gianpiero Moioli · Apress 2025

---

**Born:** 1981, Fond du Lac, Wisconsin, USA  
**Practice:** Digital art, motion graphics, VJ visuals, generative imagery  
**Key work discussed:** *Everydays: The First 5000 Days* (2021)

---

## The Everydays Project

On 1 May 2007, Mike Winkelmann — working under the name Beeple — began posting one digital image per day to his website. He has not missed a single day since. The project, called *Everydays*, grew from rough sketches into a daily visual diary combining political satire, pop-culture references, science fiction, and dystopian imagery. By 2021, it had accumulated over 5,000 works.

The entire archive was released under a **CC0 licence** — complete public domain dedication, no rights reserved. Anyone could download, remix, print, sell, or use the images without restriction or attribution. This was a deliberate choice: Beeple believed open distribution would build cultural reach faster than restriction.

---

## The Christie's Sale

On **11 March 2021**, Christie's auction house sold a single JPG file — a collage of all 5,000 *Everydays* images — as an NFT for **$69,346,250**. The buyer was Vignesh Sundaresan (known as MetaKovan), a Singapore-based cryptocurrency investor.

It was the third-highest price ever achieved at auction for a living artist, after Jeff Koons and David Hockney. It was also the first time Christie's accepted cryptocurrency (Ether) as payment.

The sale did not transfer copyright. The underlying images remained CC0. Anyone could still download them freely. What sold was the NFT: the blockchain-certified record of ownership of the original minted token.

---

## The Paradox as Argument

The Beeple case is the chapter's central demonstration that **open licensing and blockchain exclusivity are not contradictory — they are complementary**.

- CC0 maximised cultural circulation: the images spread everywhere, building the cultural aura through repetition and recognition.
- The NFT created a single, verifiable point of certified ownership within that freely circulating work.

Benjamin argued that mechanical reproduction destroys aura by detaching the work from its *hic et nunc*. Beeple's case inverts this: infinite digital reproduction builds cultural presence, while the blockchain reconstructs a new *hic et nunc* — not material and geographical, but cryptographic and transactional.
