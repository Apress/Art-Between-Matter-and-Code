---

**Born:** 1960, Padua, Italy  
**Practice:** Conceptual art, sculpture, installation, institutional critique

## Comedian (2019)

In December 2019, Cattelan presented *Comedian* at Art Basel Miami Beach: a fresh banana duct-taped directly to the gallery wall. The work was offered in an edition of three plus two artist's proofs, priced between $120,000 and $150,000 per edition. All copies sold before the fair closed.

The banana itself was not the artwork. The gallery — Perrotin — supplied a **certificate of authenticity** specifying installation instructions: buy any banana from any supermarket, tape it to any wall with any duct tape at a height of approximately 170 cm. When the banana rots, replace it. The certificate is the work. The perishable object is incidental.

During the fair, performance artist David Datuna ate one of the installed bananas. Perrotin confirmed this did not invalidate the work: the certificate remained, the banana was replaced.

## Why the Chapter Discusses It

*Comedian* is cited as a **prefiguration of NFT logic**, produced two years before the NFT market explosion of 2021.

The structural parallel is exact:

| *Comedian* (2019) | NFT (2021–) |
|-------------------|-------------|
| Certificate of authenticity = the work | NFT token = the certified ownership |
| Perishable banana = replaceable | Copyable digital file = freely reproducible |
| Instructions define the object | Smart contract defines the asset |
| Value in the certificate, not the object | Value in the token, not the file |

Cattelan did not create an object. He created a **protocol**: a set of instructions that allows any instantiation of a banana-and-tape to constitute an authentic edition of the work, provided it is accompanied by the authorised certificate.

This is precisely the logic that NFTs formalise and automate on blockchain: the certified record of authenticity is separable from, and more durable than, the underlying asset.

---

## The Institutional Dimension

*Comedian* also functions as institutional critique. The art market routinely assigns extreme monetary value to objects whose material substance is negligible — what matters is provenance, certification, and institutional validation. Cattelan made this mechanism visible by reducing the "object" to a supermarket banana.

The chapter reads this not as cynicism but as diagnosis: the art system has always traded in certificates and consensus, not solely in material. Digital art and NFTs make this explicit rather than concealed.
